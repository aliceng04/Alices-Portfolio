## 1. The goal of this case study.

Show how I scoped a marketplace into an MVP that can run real orders: which products it needs, how they connect, and the rules that decide what happens to an order, and to the money, at every step.

## 2. Problem statement.

<KeyInsight label="Problem statement">
Buyers want one place where they can buy safely and reach a wide range of products.
</KeyInsight>

## 6. Business model.

Shopay earns a commission on each **completed** order. Cancelled and fully refunded orders pay none, so the platform only makes money when the buyer gets what they paid for.

<Comparison
  columns={['Per order', 'How it is calculated', 'Example']}
  rows={[
    ['Seller base', 'Item total after product discount − shop voucher', '$100 − $10 = <strong>$90</strong>'],
    ['Buyer pays', 'Seller base − platform voucher + shipping fee', '$90 − $2 + $3 = <strong>$91</strong>'],
    ['Commission', 'Commission rate × seller base (rate per category, frozen at payment)', '5% × $90 = <strong>$4.50</strong>'],
    ['Seller payout', 'Seller base − commission (− return shipping when the seller is at fault)', '$90 − $4.50 = <strong>$85.50</strong>'],
    ['Platform keeps', 'Commission − platform voucher − card payment fee', '$4.50 − $2 − $1.82 = <strong>$0.68</strong>'],
  ]}
/>

Who pays what:

- **Shop vouchers and product discounts** are paid by the seller, so they lower the seller base and the commission with it.
- **Platform vouchers** are paid by the platform, never by the seller: the seller is still paid on the full seller base.
- **Shipping** is passed straight through to the carrier; Shopay earns nothing on it.
- **The card payment fee** is charged on everything the buyer pays and is paid by the platform. It is not returned when an order is refunded, so every refund costs the platform money.

A platform voucher bigger than the commission minus the card fee makes the order lose money, so platform vouchers run on a capped marketing budget. The commission rate (5%) and card fee (2%) in the example are placeholders until the gateway and the default rate are decided.

- **One order per shop**, so one late seller never blocks the rest of the checkout.
- **Escrow until Completed**, so a buyer can trust a shop they have never heard of.

## 10. What's next.

**Close the open questions first.** The launch market, the payment gateway and carrier, the default commission rate, and who pays return shipping when a claim is rejected. Each one changes a rule above, so none of them can wait for the build.

**Build in the order the money moves.** Each phase ends with something that can be tested end to end:

1. **Sell side:** accounts, shops, products and moderation.
2. **Buy side:** browse, cart, vouchers, checkout and card payment.
3. **Fulfilment and money:** shipping, escrow, commission and weekly payouts.
4. **After-sales:** cancellations, returns, disputes and refunds.

**Then pilot with a small group of real sellers**, and let the success metrics pick what comes next from the "Later" column: a low checkout conversion points to cash on delivery and e-wallets; a high dispute rate points to buyer-seller chat.
