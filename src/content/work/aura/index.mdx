## 1. The goal of this case study.

Show how I took a broad idea, "an app that helps ambitious people become their best selves", and turned it into a focused MVP: a clear problem, a specific user, the pains worth solving first, the solutions that solve them, and a definition of success the team can measure from day one.

## 2. Problem statement.

<KeyInsight label="Problem statement">
Ambitious 16–36 year-olds want to act on their goals, but their ambition rarely turns into consistent daily action: goals stay abstract, progress is invisible, and one bad day undoes the streak. So most of them give up, and go to bed with the same guilt.
</KeyInsight>

**What people told me.** When I talked to people about their goals, the same answer kept coming up. They wanted to start; what stopped them was not knowing where to begin, or how:

<Quote who="What I kept hearing" role="Early conversations about personal goals, paraphrased" avatar={false}>
"I want to start. I just don't know where to begin, or how."
</Quote>

Nobody was short of ambition. What they were missing was a roadmap: a clear path from the goal they had in mind to the first thing to do today.

**How big the problem is.** Research and the market point the same way: people start strong, the drop-off is steep, and they are already paying for tools that try to help.

Market-size figures vary between research firms and are directional, not precise.

**How might we** turn a vague ambition into something a person actually does today, and make the progress feel real enough to keep going?

## 5. Solutions, prioritised.

One solution per pain, ranked by what the rest of the product depends on. There is no progress to show (2) without a plan (1), and no streak to protect (3) without both.

<Decision
  eyebrow="Priority 1 · Must have"
  title="An AI that turns the goal into a plan, and the plan into today's tasks."
  image={aiPlan} alt="AI plan chat with phases and quick chips"
  labels={['Solves', 'Solution', 'Why first']}
  problem="Pain 1: people want to start but don't know where to begin, or how."
  decision="Aura works as <strong>a roadmap</strong>. The user names the goal, why it matters and what done looks like; the AI proposes phases and weekly tasks; quick chips like <strong>&quot;Shorter&quot;</strong> or <strong>&quot;4 days/week&quot;</strong> adjust it without writing a prompt. The accepted plan becomes a dated list of daily tasks."
  why="It is the foundation: every other feature reads from the plan. Without it, Aura is just another to-do list."
/>

<Decision
  eyebrow="Priority 2 · Must have"
  title="Finishing a task means capturing the moment."
  image={moment} alt="Capture moment: a photo of running shoes on stairs, tagged Tempo run · 8km, with a caption field"
  labels={['Solves', 'Solution', 'Why second']}
  problem="Pain 2: progress is invisible."
  decision="Marking a task done opens the camera. Each photo, with a short caption, is saved to the goal's journey as <strong>a small personal achievement</strong>, and the journal lets you scroll back through all of them."
  why="It is the differentiator: no to-do app or habit tracker leaves an emotional record. It only has meaning once there are tasks to complete, so it comes after the plan."
/>

**Why a photo, not a checkbox.** A ticked box disappears the moment you tick it, and a list of finished tasks says nothing about who you became along the way. I wanted every finished task to feel like an achievement in its own right, and to leave something behind. Months later, scrolling back through the journey is when you actually feel how far you have come, and that feeling is what keeps people going on the days motivation runs out.

<Decision
  eyebrow="Priority 3 · Should have"
  title="A lighter task on low-energy days, so the streak survives."
  image={swap} alt="Low energy today? sheet offering an easy 2k shakeout jog that still counts toward the streak"
  labels={['Solves', 'Solution', 'Why third']}
  problem="Pain 3: one bad day ends the attempt."
  decision="Every task offers <strong>&quot;Low energy? Swap to keep your streak&quot;</strong>: a lighter version replaces today's task and still counts toward the streak."
  why="It protects retention once people are already using the loop, and it is cheap to build. Important, but the MVP can launch without it."
/>

**How the three fit together in one day.** The plan fills today's list, the task screen keeps the *why* in view, finishing it is celebrated, and every finished task adds to the journey.

## 7. Business model.

The working price is **$4.99 a month or $39.99 a year**, with a 7-day trial: the low end of the self-improvement app range, because the audience is young and price-sensitive. It is a starting point to price-test, not a commitment, and I modelled it in a pricing and P&L sheet before putting it in the spec.

**Go-to-market, in three steps** (a plan, not results yet):

- **Pre-launch:** short-form videos on the theme *"failing at discipline"*, and a waitlist landing page that gives away Notion templates.
- **Launch:** co-create with micro-influencers in studygram, productivity and book communities; optimise the App Store listing.
- **Growth loop:** a **21-day challenge** that turns your moments into a shareable story card, so the photo ritual doubles as marketing; invite two friends to the challenge and get a month of Premium free.

## 9. What's next & what I learned.

**What's next.** The roadmap runs in three phases, each unlocked by proof from the one before:

<Comparison
  columns={['Phase', 'Focus', 'What it adds']}
  rows={[
    ['1 · MVP & acquire', 'Prove the loop works', 'The core app, onboarding to a first goal, reminders, and a way to collect feedback from the first users'],
    ['2 · Growth & retention', 'Keep people coming back', 'Ongoing AI coaching, custom themes, the referral programme, and the first paid challenges and templates'],
    ['3 · Personalisation', 'Make it feel made for you', 'Plans that adapt to your energy each day, and a loyalty programme for long-term users'],
  ]}
/>

Before phase 2, the three bets underneath the MVP have to hold: AI plans are good enough to follow, the photo ritual feels like a reward rather than a chore, and people will pay for Premium. The first step is structured interviews with people like Maya, followed by a prototype test of the core loop.

**What I learned.**

- **Product sense is mostly subtraction.** The most useful decisions were about what the MVP is *not*: no social feed, no coaches, no marketplace. One loop, done well, beats ten features done halfway.
- **Start from what people say, not from the screens.** The project began with a design export; the clearest direction came later, from people telling me they wanted to start but didn't know how.
- **Be honest about what is still a bet.** Writing down the assumptions, with how I would test each one, made the plan more credible, not less.
